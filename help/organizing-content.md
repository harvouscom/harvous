# Organizing Content

Learn how to organize your Bible study notes effectively in Harvous. This guide covers organization strategies, workflows, and best practices.

## The "For You" Inbox System

### What is "For You"?

"For You" is your inbox - a capture zone for quick note-taking and unorganized content:

- **Notes not in a thread yet**: Shown in the My Pile thread until you add them elsewhere
- **Threads not in a space yet**: Threads that aren't assigned to a space
- **Perfect for**: Rapid idea capture during study
- **Later organization**: Move items from "For You" to organized spaces when ready

### Using "For You"

1. **Quick capture**: Create notes quickly without worrying about organization
2. **Review regularly**: Periodically review "For You" to organize content
3. **Move to spaces**: Assign threads to spaces when ready
4. **Move to threads**: Assign notes to threads when ready

### "Full List" Tab

The "Full List" tab shows your complete organizational structure:

- **All spaces**: See every space you've created
- **Thread hierarchies**: See threads within each space
- **Item counts**: See how many items are in each space/thread
- **Complete overview**: Great for finding and reviewing past studies

## Moving Content

### Moving Notes Between Threads

1. **Open the note** you want to move
2. **Click the "More" menu** (three dots)
3. **Select "Edit"** or open the details panel
4. **Choose a new thread** from the dropdown
5. **Save your changes**

The note will move to the new thread, but you can also add it to multiple threads (see Many-to-Many Relationships below).

### Moving Threads Between Spaces

1. **Open the thread** you want to move
2. **Click the "More" menu** (three dots)
3. **Select "Edit"**
4. **Choose a new space** from the dropdown
5. **Save your changes**

The thread will move to the new space, and all its notes will move with it.

### Moving Individual Notes

Notes can exist directly in a space (not in any thread):

1. **Create a note** and leave it unorganized
2. **Or remove a note from all threads** - it becomes unorganized
3. **Add it to a space** by assigning it to a thread later

## Many-to-Many Relationships

One of Harvous's most powerful features is that **notes can belong to multiple threads**:

### How It Works

- **Add to multiple threads**: A note can belong to several threads at once
- **No duplication**: The note isn't copied - it's the same note in multiple places
- **Remove from one**: Removing from one thread doesn't affect other threads
- **Flexible organization**: Content isn't locked into rigid categories

### Example Use Cases

**Cross-Referencing**
- A note about "Faith" might belong to both "Romans Study" and "Personal Growth"
- A note about "Prayer" might belong to both "Prayer Journal" and "Sermon Notes"

**Topic Overlap**
- Bible study topics naturally overlap
- Many-to-many relationships reflect this reality
- You can find the same note from different contexts

### How to Add a Note to Multiple Threads

1. **Open the note** you want to add to multiple threads
2. **Click the "More" menu** (three dots)
3. **Select "Edit"** or open the details panel
4. **Select multiple threads** from the thread selector
5. **Save your changes**

The note will now appear in all selected threads.

## Organization Strategies

### Top-Down Organization

Start with the big picture and work down:

1. **Create spaces** for major study areas (Bible Study, Prayer Journal, etc.)
2. **Create threads** within spaces for specific topics
3. **Add notes** to threads as you study

**Best for**: People who like structure and planning ahead

### Bottom-Up Organization

Start with notes and organize later:

1. **Create notes** quickly in "For You" without worrying about organization
2. **Create threads** as patterns emerge in your notes
3. **Create spaces** to group related threads
4. **Move content** from "For You" to organized spaces when ready

**Best for**: People who prefer to capture first and organize later

### Hybrid Approach

Mix both methods:

1. **Create some spaces** for major areas you know you'll need
2. **Capture notes** quickly without always organizing immediately
3. **Let organization grow** naturally as your study evolves
4. **Reorganize** as needed - nothing is permanent

**Best for**: Most people - flexible and adaptable

## Example Workflows

### Daily Bible Reading

1. **Create a "Daily Reading" thread** in your "Bible Study" space
2. **Add notes** for each day's reading (N001, N002, N003...)
3. **Include**: Scripture references, key insights, questions, applications
4. **Review periodically** to see patterns and growth

### Sermon Notes

1. **Create a "Sermon Notes" space**
2. **Add threads** for different series or time periods
   - "January 2024 Sermons"
   - "February 2024 Sermons"
   - "Special Services"
3. **Take notes** during services, then organize by thread
4. **Review later** to reinforce what you learned

### Prayer Journal

1. **Create a "Prayer Journal" space**
2. **Add threads** for different categories:
   - "Daily Prayers" - regular prayer entries
   - "Prayer Requests" - requests from others
   - "Thanksgivings" - answered prayers
3. **Date your notes** and track how prayers are answered
4. **Reflect on patterns** in your prayer life

### Bible Study Group

1. **Create a "Church Small Group" space** (shared)
2. **Add threads** for different study topics:
   - "Romans Study Discussion"
   - "Group Prayer Requests"
   - "Meeting Notes"
3. **Collaborate** with group members (coming soon)
4. **Share insights** and learn together

## Best Practices

### Naming Conventions

- **Be consistent**: Use similar patterns for similar content
- **Be descriptive**: "Gospel of John Study" is better than "John"
- **Include context**: "Sunday Sermons - January 2024" helps you find it later
- **Keep it simple**: Don't overthink names - clarity is key

### Organization Tips

1. **Start simple**: Don't create too many spaces or threads at once
2. **Let it grow**: Add new organization as your study needs evolve
3. **Regular cleanup**: Periodically review "For You" and organize content
4. **Use colors**: Choose colors that help you quickly identify spaces and threads
5. **Review and refine**: Your organization will evolve - that's okay!

### Content Creation Tips

1. **Capture first**: Don't worry about perfect organization when creating notes
2. **Organize later**: Move content from "For You" when you have time
3. **Use multiple threads**: Add notes to multiple threads when relevant
4. **Review regularly**: Check "For You" weekly to keep it organized

## Troubleshooting Organization

### "I have too many unorganized notes"

- **Set aside time**: Schedule regular organization sessions
- **Start with threads**: Create threads for common topics, then move notes
- **Use search**: Search for keywords to find related notes
- **Don't overthink**: Sometimes "good enough" organization is better than perfect

### "I don't know where to put this note"

- **Put it in "For You"**: You can always organize it later
- **Add to multiple threads**: If it fits multiple topics, add it to all of them
- **Create a new thread**: If a new topic emerges, create a thread for it
- **Keep it simple**: Don't create complex hierarchies - simple is better

### "My organization feels messy"

- **That's okay**: Organization is a process, not a destination
- **Reorganize when needed**: Nothing is permanent - you can always move things
- **Focus on content**: The organization serves your study, not the other way around
- **Start fresh**: Sometimes it's easier to reorganize than to fix a messy system
