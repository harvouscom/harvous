AI can summarize a passage in seconds. That can be a useful pointer—or the wrong kind of help.

Notes-first means the primary artifact of study is your encounter with the text: captured, kept, connected—not a generated explanation that replaces the encounter. You still use commentaries and friends. Humans help humans. The tool shouldn’t pretend autocomplete is the same as attending to Scripture.

AI can help with a half-remembered cross-reference or a schedule you’ll rewrite. It flatters when it hands you applications and “deep” group questions before you’ve sat with the text. Friction isn’t always the enemy.

The unofficial rules for AI apps for Christians say it plainly: don’t replace human relationships or spiritual practices; don’t fabricate Scripture; stay honest about what the tool is.

Proverbs 25:2 stays near our posture (not as a full tech theology): it is the glory of God to conceal a matter; to search out a matter is the glory of kings. See Proverbs 25:2. Tools should serve the searching—not replace the searcher.

Rule of thumb: Read first. Ask for a pointer, not a substitute encounter. Keep a note in your own words afterward.
