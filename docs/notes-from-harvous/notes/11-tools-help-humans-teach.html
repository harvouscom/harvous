Humans help humans. Tools only help.

That sentence is a boundary. Near Scripture, tools are tempted to do the holy parts: summarize so nobody struggles, generate the lesson so the tired teacher sleeps, replace the mentor with a feed. Speed isn’t evil. Speed that removes the human search can still be a loss.

Proverbs 25:2 sits near how we think about this: it is the glory of God to conceal a matter; to search out a matter is the glory of kings. See Proverbs 25:2. The tool should serve the search, not replace the searcher.

Education for the church means believers keep what they learn, teachers leave trails, groups continue across weeks, and churches can eventually organize continuity—without an AI pastor, a content mill that displaces local teachers, or a machine that treats the gathered body as optional.

Notes are the right primitive because they’re humble. A note doesn’t preach for you. It keeps the thought you almost lost—next to the verse it belongs to.

The faith.tools guidance lands in the same neighborhood: don’t replace relationships or practices; don’t fake Scripture; stay a tool.

Let tools carry memory. Let people carry care. Let Scripture carry authority. Let the church remain a body.

Longer read: Tools help. Humans teach.
