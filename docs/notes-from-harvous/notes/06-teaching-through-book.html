Teaching one lesson is hard. Teaching through a book is a different sport: you’re holding an arc across weeks while life interrupts half the group.

Book studies usually fail because the container is weak—no map of where you’ve been, no shared “so far,” prep that treats every week as an episode.

Before week one, write a spine: book in one plain sentence; the arc you hope people feel; how you’ll chunk it; how newcomers catch up; where series notes will live.

Two series habits:

  • So far opener (3 min) — retrieve the path. This is retrieval practice in church clothes.
  • Handoff closer (2 min) — one sentence to still have next week.

Organize at two levels: a series thread (James; Mark) and passage notes for each week’s text. When week nine asks “didn’t we talk about trials?” you can find James 1:2–4.

When you get behind, update the spine honestly. Don’t speed-run people for a syllabus grade.
