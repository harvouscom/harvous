You taught a solid lesson. People nodded. Something landed. By Wednesday the takeaway has softened into “something about grace,” and you’re prepping next week like last week never happened.

That fade usually isn’t a people problem. Learning lasts when we retrieve what we heard—not only when we hear it once. Church life is rich in exposure and thin on return.

What evaporates first: structure, connections, questions, then the personal edge. What’s left is vague warmth. Warmth isn’t nothing—but it’s hard to build continuity on warmth alone.

Teachers need a trail: the passage, one sentence you don’t want to lose, one midweek question, a note on what the room actually wrestled with. Without a trail, every Sunday is a standalone event.

This isn’t homework for everyone. It isn’t a ChMS. Humans still teach humans. A trail just means good teaching has somewhere to live when the chairs are stacked.

Try tonight: Write the passage, one Wednesday sentence, and one question. Put them where you’ll open them again—ideally next to the verses.
