A good question opens a passage. A bad one closes a room. You can practice the craft without a personality transplant.

In class or group, a question should help people look at the text again, think with it (not around it), and speak honestly. If it can be answered with the Bible closed, it may be pastoral—but know which kind you’re asking.

Three kinds that earn their place:

  • Observation — What do you notice? Repeated words? Who is speaking?
  • Meaning — What is this saying in context? What would first hearers find hard?
  • Response — What is one honest carry this week—proportionate, not a five-point life overhaul?

Write questions before you improvise. Prefer fewer. Let the text referee when answers diverge. After class, capture the live question from the third row—that’s how craft compounds.

Don’t use questions as traps (“guess what I’m thinking”). Don’t demand vulnerability from people who just met. Psalm 139:23–24 is a prayer of honest searching before God—not a group method—but the tone still helps: real looking without spectacle. See Psalm 139:23–24.
