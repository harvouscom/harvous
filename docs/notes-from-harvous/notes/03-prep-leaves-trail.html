Most lesson prep is built for delivery: outline, story, question, timing. Then class ends and the prep has nowhere to go. Next week starts cold.

Aim for a second layer: trail prep. Not only how will I teach this hour? but what will still be findable later?

A simple flow:

  1. Passage first — write references with verses (Romans 8:1–4, not “Romans 8”).
  2. One-sentence aim — if you can’t say it in one line, the trail lacks a spine.
  3. Two questions — one observation, one meaning-or-response. Capture the live question after class too.
  4. Blank zone: what actually happened — three minutes after class. Script vs living thread.

Curriculum binders can help with structure. Your trail holds what your people needed. Scripture-linked notes help because you return by text, not by a filename invented while tired.

Before your next class, make one note with four headings: Passage / Aim / Two questions / After class. Teach from it. Fill #4 before you sleep.

Longer read: Prep a lesson that leaves a trail.
