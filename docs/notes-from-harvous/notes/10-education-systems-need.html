When churches say “education system,” the talk often jumps to software: rosters, PDFs, integrations. Those can matter. They’re easy to confuse with the job—helping people learn Scripture together over time.

Healthy systems need: a living trail of teaching; continuity across sermon, class, and group; return built into the week; clear ownership with light bureaucracy; honesty about mixed stages of faith.

They don’t need: to become your ChMS; AI to manufacture discipleship; infinite content libraries; holiness tracking.

Audit questions (no software required): Where does last quarter’s trail live—really? Can a new teacher inherit more than a dusty binder? Do Sunday and midweek share a bridge? What happens Monday after a strong Sunday? Which tools help humans teach—and which ask humans to serve the tool?

Harvous today helps individuals and teachers keep scripture-linked notes—the prep and personal layer of the trail. Shared spaces and church-level continuity are directions we’re building toward, not features to overclaim. We’re not a ChMS. We’re not replacing the body.

Start with principles: one-sentence aims, passage-linked trails, Monday return, Sunday→midweek bridges. Software may amplify those later; it can’t invent them.
