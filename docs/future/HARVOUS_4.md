# Harvous 4

> **Status:** thinking-out-loud, not a ship date. Written so the four products stay in one picture before any of them get built in isolation.
>
> **Related:** [REVIEWS_CHALLENGES_SEASON_PASS_STRATEGY.md](./REVIEWS_CHALLENGES_SEASON_PASS_STRATEGY.md) (canonical Review / Challenges / Season Pass thesis), [CHALLENGES_AS_SUGGESTIONS.md](./CHALLENGES_AS_SUGGESTIONS.md) (why personal Challenges was withheld), [MONETIZATION_AND_PRICING.md](./MONETIZATION_AND_PRICING.md), [CHURCH_ORG_AND_CURRICULUM.md](./CHURCH_ORG_AND_CURRICULUM.md), [STUDY_PLANS.md](./STUDY_PLANS.md), [HARVOUS_SDK_AND_FUTURE_ROADMAP.md](./HARVOUS_SDK_AND_FUTURE_ROADMAP.md).

Four plans. One home for Bible study that remembers.

## Why four

Harvous 3 already has the loop: write a note, keep Scripture in the flow, thread what belongs together, let Suggestions bring a fading thought back. Plus already unlocks Review and hosting Shared Spaces. Challenges is already named on the public roadmap as time-boxed work, solo or with others.

Harvous 4 is not a fifth product. It is the moment those pieces become four clear plans people can name — and the moment Review stops being a private drill and becomes a season the church and the public can walk through together.

The four plans are **Personal** (free), **Plus**, **Church**, and **Challenges**. Personal stays the home for a single student’s notes. Plus stays the paid layer for Review and hosting rooms. Church is the plan that treats a congregation as a first-class host. Challenges is the open season — public by default, hosted in shared spaces, optionally hosted by a church, and built so Review becomes a curated, timed test instead of a lone quiz.

## The four plans

| Plan | Who it is for | What they get | What they do not lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | Anyone studying the Bible. No card. | Unlimited notes, pills, highlights, threads, @ mentions, reminders, Daily Passage, joining spaces. | The whole core app. Challenges they can join. A recap study guide after a season. |
| **Plus** | The student who wants Review and rooms of their own. | Review. Host Shared Spaces (cover, threads, up to 12). Deeper recall history. | Everything in Personal. Can run a small group challenge inside a space they host. Pre-challenge study guide when they already have a Harvous home. |
| **Church** | A congregation, campus, or ministry that wants to host study at scale. | Church Space(s), member roles, seasonal Challenge hosting, branded cover, staff Review packs, reporting that stays pastoral — not surveillance. | Members keep Personal (or Plus) homes. Church never swallows private notes. |
| **Challenges** | The public. Existing users. Churches that want a season with a finish line. | Open, time-boxed seasons. Challenge mode from a persistent dock. Curated timed tests. Practice sittings. Study guides before (existing users) and after (everyone). | Joining is free. Hosting a public season may sit on Plus or Church. Your private notes stay yours. |

## Personal — still free

Personal is the promise that must not move. Unlimited notes. Scripture pills in the translations Harvous already carries. Highlights and annotations. Folders that file themselves and threads you connect by hand. @ mentions. Reminders. Daily Passage. Search. Offline and sync. Joining a Shared Space is always free.

In Harvous 4, Personal also means: you can enter a public Challenge from the dock, sit the timed test in Challenge mode (guest or signed in), and keep a recap study guide when the window closes. Existing signed-in users also get a **pre-challenge study guide** while the season is still open, so they can practice before they sit. You should not need Plus to join a public sitting or to keep either guide. Plus is for hosting and for Review that is not bound to a public season.

- **Guardrail:** no artificial cap that makes the free home feel like a trial of the paid one.
- **Guardrail:** private notes never leak into a Challenge space unless the writer places them there.
- **Guardrail:** both study guides — the pre-challenge pack and the post-challenge recap — live in the person’s own library, not only in the season room.

## Plus — Review and rooms

Plus stays the individual paid plan. The current shape is already right: Review (write an answer from memory, then say how it went, from your own notes and verses) and hosting Shared Spaces — unlimited spaces, up to 12 people each, joining always free.

Harvous 4 should deepen Plus without turning it into Church-lite:

- **Own-note Review packs.** Review packs you build from your own threads — a set of prompts that follow a book, a topic, or a sermon series you already wrote through.
- **Host a private season.** A Plus user can host a closed Challenge inside a space they own: same Review engine, smaller room, not listed in the public season catalog.
- **Recall history that is yours.** How a note, highlight, or verse came back; what you marked easy or hard. Useful later when a public Challenge builds a test from similar material.

Pricing can stay in the neighborhood of today’s Plus ($6/mo or $36/yr founding). Church should not be a Plus multiplier sold to one person. It is a different customer.

## Church — the congregation as host

Church is the missing named plan. Shared Spaces already let a group share a room. Church is what happens when that room is a ministry, not a friend group: more than one space, roles that match real church life (pastor / teacher / group leader / member / guest), a cover that looks like the church rather than a generic room, and the ability to host a seasonal Challenge for the body — or to adopt a public Challenge as “ours this quarter.”

### What Church is for

- Sermon series that leave a trail in a shared space instead of disappearing into fifty Apple Notes.
- Small groups that pick up next week where this week stopped.
- A church-wide Challenge (Lent, Advent, “the Gospel of John this fall”) with a curated timed test and study guides the body can keep — practice beforehand, recap afterward.
- Staff who need to prepare Review prompts from the series, not from a stranger’s public pack.

### What Church is not

- Not a church management system. No giving, check-in, or membership database. Harvous remains a notes home.
- Not a surveillance product. Pastors see participation at the level a good small-group leader would — who joined the season, who finished a sitting — not the private notes in a member’s Personal space, and not a keystroke log of what they opened mid-question.
- Not a content marketplace that replaces the pastor’s voice. The church writes or adopts the season. Harvous supplies the room, Challenge mode, and the study-guide output.

### Membership and notes

Every church member still has a Personal home. The Church Space is a shared drawer, not a replacement drawer. A note can live in both when the writer threads it that way. Default is private. Share is a choice.

## Challenges — the fourth product

Challenges is the plan that celebrates the other three at once. It is open to the public. It uses Shared Spaces as the room. A church can host one. And it takes Review from a private practice into a seasonal, **timed**, curated test.

This is the public face of what [REVIEWS_CHALLENGES_SEASON_PASS_STRATEGY.md](./REVIEWS_CHALLENGES_SEASON_PASS_STRATEGY.md) called Season Pass, without requiring a separate consumer SKU on day one. Joining stays free. The paid acts are *hosting* (Plus or Church) and *unbounded personal Review* (Plus).

### The job of a Challenge

A Challenge is a season with a published open window. Inside that window people enter **Challenge mode** and sit a timed test. Outside that window — and beside it, for practice — they use study guides and test challenges so the live sitting is not the first time they have seen the material.

Not a streak counter. Not a reading plan that forgets you wrote anything. The path is study. The sitting is timed Review you can actually take.

### Challenge mode

Challenge mode is a focused guest-or-member experience, not a different app.

- **Who.** A guest can sit a public Challenge without an account. A signed-in member sits the same Challenge with their home still underneath — notes, threads, Scripture they already keep.
- **How you get there.** A persistent dock item for the active Challenge. It is timed: it appears when the season opens, it counts the remaining window, and it leaves when the window closes. One obvious door. Not buried in settings.
- **What it feels like.** The rest of Harvous is still reachable. Challenge mode does not lock the library. If you leave a question to open a note, a thread, a chapter, or a highlight, that exploration is part of the sitting.
- **Per-question activity.** Each question has its own clock inside the larger timed Challenge. What you visit during that question is tracked and taken into account — which passage you opened, which note or guide page you returned to, whether you stayed on the prompt. The point is honesty about how the answer was formed, not a surveillance score. Guests see only this sitting. Members can later see their own trail beside the recap guide.
- **The whole Challenge is timed.** The season has an open/close. The sitting has a duration. Questions have their own beats. When time is up, the sitting ends. That is why the guides and the practice tests exist: so timed is fair, not ambush.

### Two study guides, not one

Study guides are first-class artifacts. There are two moments, on purpose.

1. **Pre-challenge study guide — existing Harvous users.**
   While the Challenge is open, anyone who already has a Harvous home (signed in, Personal or Plus or Church member) gets a study guide *before* they sit. It is the season’s map for practice: passages, host prompts, the shape of the test, and — where it helps — pointers into notes and threads they already wrote that touch this season. This is the advantage of already living in Harvous. New or guest sitters can still take practice tests; they do not get a personalized pre-guide until they have a home to hang it on.

2. **Recap study guide — any Harvous user, now or later.**
   When the sitting ends, every participant can save a recap for future learning: the passage map, the host prompts, their answers, the questions they missed, and a short trail of what they visited during each question. Guests who create an account after the fact can claim that recap. Future Harvous users who missed the live window can still receive the published recap for the season — the public study guide — so the Challenge keeps teaching after the dock item is gone.

Both guides save like notes. They belong to the person. They are not trapped in the season room.

### Why we publish guides and offer test challenges

Because the live Challenge is timed, practice has to be a product, not a footnote.

- **Published study guides** let people learn the season on their own clock — before, during, and after the open window.
- **Test challenges** are untimed or loosely timed practice sittings on the same curated questions (or a sibling set). Same Challenge mode, same dock pattern when a practice window is offered, no public ranking. Use them to learn the shape of a question before the clock matters.
- Existing users study the pre-guide, run a test challenge, then sit the live window. Guests can run a test challenge, sit live, then keep the recap if they stay.

That is the lift Review has been waiting for. Review on Plus is “quiz yourself from what you happened to write.” Challenges is “this season chose the text; the clock is real; you were given a guide and a practice sitting; now sit; then keep the recap.”

### How a season is built

- **Host.** A public catalog season (Harvous-hosted or church-hosted but open) or a closed season inside a Plus or Church space.
- **Window.** Published open and close. The dock item is bound to this window.
- **Curriculum.** Passages, a suggested weekly rhythm, host-written threads, optional templates. Prefer existing study-plan sequences (`STUDY_PLANS.md`) over a new content type. The pre-challenge guide is compiled from this.
- **Room.** The Shared Space for the season. Joining is free. Writing in the space is writing in Harvous — pills, highlights, threads work the same.
- **Challenge mode + curated test.** Timed sitting. Questions drawn from the season’s passages, the host’s notes and prompts, and (if the sitter opts in) notes they filed during the weeks. Activity during each question is part of the record of that sitting.
- **Practice track.** At least one test challenge published beside the live window.
- **Guides.** Pre-challenge guide for existing users as soon as the season is announced or opens. Recap guide for every sitter when their sitting ends, plus a public recap for people who arrive later.

### Public and church, together

A Challenge can be listed publicly so anyone — guest or Personal — can join from the dock. A Church plan holder can host that listing, or clone it into a members-only room. A Plus user can run a quiet version for a group of twelve. Same engine. Three doors.

### Tone

Challenges should feel like a well-taught class, not a leaderboard. Optional cohort presence (who else is in this week’s sitting) is enough. Rankings, public scores, and shame mechanics stay out — consistent with the 3.0 promise of no score, no streak, and no leaderboard. Per-question activity is for the sitter’s recap and for making timed practice honest. It is not a public grade. The glory is searching the matter — not beating the person next to you.

## How the four plans touch each other

Think of one object model, four doors:

- Note, highlight, thread, folder, space — unchanged from Harvous 3.
- Review item — already Plus. In 4, a Review item can belong to a person, to a space, or to a Challenge season.
- Season — new. Has an open window, a space, a prompt set, a dock binding, a live sitting and a practice sitting.
- Challenge mode — new session. Guest or member. Timed. Per-question activity attached to the sitting, not to the person’s private life outside the sitting.
- Study guide — two artifacts. Pre-challenge (existing users). Recap (any user, including future users of a published season). Note-like, owned by the participant.

Personal can enter Challenge mode and keep recaps. Existing Personal/Plus/Church members also receive the pre-challenge guide. Plus can host small seasons and build Review from private notes. Church can host large seasons and staff the prompts. Challenges is the public face of seasons — the dock item, the clock, the guides, the practice tests.

## What we will not do in 4

- Replace the Bible reader people already love. Harvous stays the notes home beside it.
- Center AI-written devotionals. Connector can wait. Challenges are curated by hosts, not generated as filler.
- Turn Church into ChMS.
- Paywall joining a public Challenge, sitting as a guest, or keeping a recap from a season you finished.
- Show pastors a member’s Personal notes, or publish per-question exploration as a public score.
- Ship native Apple apps as a dependency of 4. They can land on their own track.

## A possible sequence, not a calendar

This is order of learning, not a promise of quarters.

1. Name the four plans in public language. Personal / Plus / Church / Challenges. Even if Church is “coming,” the map should be visible.
2. Make Review items attachable to a space, not only to a person. That is the bridge from Plus Review to a hosted test.
3. Ship Challenge mode as a dock item bound to one Harvous-hosted public window (one book, 4–6 weeks), with a practice test challenge beside it.
4. Ship the two guides for that prototype: pre-challenge for signed-in users when the window is announced, recap for every sitter. Measure: dock opens, practice-test starts, live finish rate, whether people save and reopen the recap.
5. Let Plus hosts clone that prototype into a private space.
6. Open Church as a plan when a real congregation wants to host the next season — not before. Design roles with that first church, not in the abstract.
7. Only then build the catalog: upcoming seasons, past recaps, “adopt this as our church’s fall.”

## Success, said plainly

Harvous 4 works if a person who already takes notes can point to a season and say: I had a guide before the clock started, I practiced, I sat the timed test, and I still have the recap. It works if a guest can find the dock, sit, and keep learning afterward. It works if a church can host that season without becoming a software project. It works if Personal is still free and still enough to study every day.

Four plans. The same home. A season worth finishing — and a guide that outlasts the dock.

Keep your Bible app. Add Harvous. In 4, add a season worth sitting.
