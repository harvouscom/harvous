What happened that day

Good Friday is named well. Something terrible and something important happened, and the Christian tradition has kept this day in the calendar for nearly two thousand years—not to dwell on violence, but because you can't understand Sunday without it.

Here's the basic shape of the day.

Jesus was arrested the night before in Gethsemane, Luke 22:47-54. By early morning he was before Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor. Pilate found no basis for execution—John 18:38 records him saying so three times—but the crowd's pressure held, and he handed Jesus over. Mark 15:15.

The crucifixion

Roman crucifixion wasn't just execution. It was public humiliation designed to send a message: this person is an enemy of the empire, stripped of dignity, displayed near a road where people could see. The cross was reserved for the worst offenders—rebels, bandits, slaves who revolted. Crucifying someone was a statement about who they were.

The Gospel of Mark tells it plainly. He was crucified at the third hour—roughly 9 in the morning. Mark 15:25. There was darkness over the whole land from noon until 3pm. Mark 15:33. Then Jesus cried out—"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"—quoting the opening line of Psalm 22:1. And he died. Mark 15:37.

Matthew notes the temple curtain tore in two from top to bottom, Matthew 27:51. That curtain separated the holiest space from the rest of the temple. What exactly that means has been discussed for centuries. But Mark records that a Roman soldier—a Gentile, an outsider—looked up and said: "Surely this man was the Son of God." Mark 15:39.

A short reading for today

If you have 10 minutes, read Mark 15:1-39. It's spare, almost journalistic. Mark doesn't editorialize. He just says what happened.

Or, if you want something shorter, read just Mark 15:33-39—the final six verses. The darkness, the cry, and the soldier's words.

One thing to do with this

Resist the urge to skip to Sunday. That's an instinct—especially if you know how the story ends—but the disciples didn't know. They sat in confusion and grief on Saturday with no idea what was coming.

If you read the passage today, notice what catches your eye. You can write it down or just carry it through the day. That's enough for a Friday.
