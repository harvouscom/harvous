Science, history, and resurrection claims

Science is remarkably good at repeatable patterns in nature—physics, chemistry, biology, medicine. Christians and non-Christians alike rely on it every day. Psalm 19:1 and Colossians 1:16–17 are examples of Scripture celebrating God as the source of order in creation, without turning every verse into a lab report.

The resurrection, as Christians describe it, is not a routine natural event you’d expect to reproduce in a lab. That doesn’t end conversation; it steers us toward the tools that fit: history—sources and context; philosophy—what counts as evidence for a one-off event; and trust—whether this person or community bears witness in a way worth weighing.

Sometimes research illuminates the story’s setting—archaeology sketching the first-century world you meet in Luke and Acts. That’s different from saying “science measured the resurrection.” Honest wording keeps trust with thoughtful newcomers and skeptics alike.

For Christians in science who take both faith and scientific integrity seriously, explore the American Scientific Affiliation (ASA).
