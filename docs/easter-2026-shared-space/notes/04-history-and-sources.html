History and sources

The New Testament—especially the Gospels, Acts, and letters like Romans and 1 Corinthians—is our earliest Christian testimony to Jesus’ death and the claim that God raised him. Historians ask how close these texts sit to the events, how they cohere or differ, what we can say with confidence, and what stays debated.

Many scholars agree on basics: Jesus was crucified under Pontius Pilate—Mark 15:1–47 and John 19:1–42 align with first-century context, and non-Christian writers such as Tacitus also point in that direction. His followers soon proclaimed he had been raised; Acts 2:1–47 and Acts 13:26–33 show that message spreading in public speech, not only private devotion.

There is wide disagreement on how to explain the experiences behind that claim—vision, legend, miracle, and other proposals all appear in the literature. Good history names that range instead of pretending everyone agrees.

Paul cites what many read as an early summary in 1 Corinthians 15:3–8—a helpful example of how quickly the resurrection claim circulated in first communities, with named witnesses in some traditions.

We are not saying “history proves the resurrection” the way a repeatable experiment might. We are saying the claim has a real historical context, real texts, and real debates—worth reading charitably and critically.
