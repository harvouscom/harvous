Start with Paul’s argument

Before the headings, read 1 Corinthians 15:12–22 in a translation you like. Paul is writing to a real church that had begun to wonder whether “raised” language was only metaphor. He doesn’t dodge the question.

He walks the logic step by step: if the dead are not raised, then Christ has not been raised—and preaching and faith collapse into error. If Christ has been raised, he is the firstfruits of those who have died, and the whole hope of the chapter unfolds from there. Your Bible’s wording will carry the emotional weight better than any summary here.

What many Christians say follows

  • Hope — death is not the end of God’s purpose for us. 1 Thessalonians 4:13–14 speaks to grief with resurrection hope, not empty cheer.
  • Forgiveness and reconciliation — the cross and resurrection together are often described as God dealing with sin and drawing people home. Romans 4:24–25 ties faith to the God who “raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.”
  • New life now — not only life after death, but a different rhythm here—love, justice, and peace as echoes of God’s kingdom. Colossians 3:1–4 invites setting heart and mind on what is above because Christ is seated at God’s right hand.

1 Peter 1:3 speaks of a “living hope” through Jesus’ resurrection—language many churches still reach for on Easter.
