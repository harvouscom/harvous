One arc, plain language

Easter is the Christian celebration of Jesus rising from the dead after his crucifixion. The story Christians tell follows a sequence you can trace in the Gospels—for example, Mark across Mark 14:1–72, Mark 15:1–47, and Mark 16:1–20; Matthew across Matthew 26:1–75, Matthew 27:1–66, and Matthew 28:1–20; Luke across Luke 22:1–71, Luke 23:1–56, and Luke 24:1–53; John across John 18:1–40, John 19:1–42, John 20:1–31, and John 21:1–25:

  1. Jesus’ ministry — teaching, healing, and announcing that God’s kingdom is breaking in, as in Mark 1:14–15.
  2. Passion — arrest, trial, crucifixion, death, and burial, often remembered on Good Friday; John 19:16–30 is one stark telling.
  3. Empty tomb and appearances — followers find the tomb empty and report seeing Jesus alive—compare Luke 24:1–12 and John 20:1–18.
  4. Meaning — sin and death don’t get the last word; new life with God is possible. Romans 6:4–5 connects baptism imagery to walking in “newness of life” with Christ.

Four Gospels, one story-shaped memory. They don’t match in every detail; historians and pastors talk openly about that. Still, the core claim runs through all of them: resurrection—not merely that Jesus’ ideas live on, but that God raised Jesus. How exactly to describe his risen life is something thoughtful people still discuss.

Paul’s summary in 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 shows early Christians repeating the same claim in a compact, creed-like form—worth reading beside any Gospel ending. Peter’s speech in Acts 2:22–24 ties the resurrection to God’s action in front of the same world that crucified Jesus.

For a neutral overview of how Easter fits in Christian belief and history, see the Britannica article on the resurrection of Jesus—encyclopedia summary, not devotional.
