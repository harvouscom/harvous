Week 4: Love

Main passage: Matthew 1:18-25.

Matthew tells the Christmas story from Joseph's side. Joseph learns that Mary, his betrothed, is pregnant, and he plans to end the engagement quietly so as not to shame her. Then an angel tells him in a dream that the child is from the Holy Spirit, and that he will save his people from their sins. Joseph wakes and does what he is told.

Matthew sees in this the word given through Isaiah: "Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel," which means God with us (Matthew 1:23, quoting Isaiah 7:14). Isaiah first gave that sign to King Ahaz in a crisis of his own, and readers differ on how it spoke to his day. Matthew sees its fullest meaning in Jesus. That is the shape of Christmas love. God does not send a message from far away. He comes near, in a body, into a family under strain.

John says it plainly: "In this the love of God was made manifest among us, that God sent his only Son into the world, so that we might live through him" (1 John 4:9). Love here is not first a feeling we have. It is what God did.

Read this week

  • Day 2: Isaiah 7:10-14. The sign of Immanuel, first given to a frightened king.
  • Day 3: Luke 1:26-38. Gabriel comes to Mary: "Let it be to me according to your word."
  • Day 4: Hosea 11:1-4. God's love for his people, like a parent teaching a child to walk.
  • Day 5: John 3:16-21. God so loved the world.
  • Day 6: 1 John 4:7-19. We love because he first loved us.
  • Day 7: Romans 8:31-39. Nothing can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus.

Questions to sit with

  • Joseph's love looks like quiet obedience and protection. Where is love asking something practical of you this week?
  • What changes when you read "God with us" as a description of your week, not only of Bethlehem?
  • 1 John 4 says love starts with God. How does that change the way you think about loving people who are hard to love?
