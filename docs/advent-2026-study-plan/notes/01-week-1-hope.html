Before you start

Advent is the four weeks before Christmas. The word means "coming," and the season holds two comings at once: the birth of Jesus, which the church remembers, and his return, which the church still waits for. So Advent is not only a countdown to a holiday. It is practice in waiting well.

Each week of this plan has one main passage, six short readings for the days that follow, a few questions, and one thing to watch or read if you want to go further. Start any day. If you fall behind, pick up at this week's step and keep going; nobody is grading.

Many churches light a candle each Sunday of Advent and give each week a word. This plan uses hope, peace, joy and love, which is common in many Protestant churches. Other traditions name the weeks differently (the prophets, Bethlehem, the shepherds, the angels), and some keep the third Sunday as a Sunday of joy. The Scriptures are the same either way.

Short quotations in this plan are from the English Standard Version (ESV). Read along in whichever translation you use.

Week 1: Hope

Main passage: Isaiah 9:2-7.

Isaiah spoke to a people facing invasion and exile. The northern kingdom of Israel was being overrun by Assyria, and the land around Galilee was among the first to fall. Into that darkness Isaiah announced a light: a child born, a son given, whose rule would have no end.

Notice what kind of hope this is. It is not optimism, the sense that things are likely to get better. It is a promise that rests on God's own commitment: "The zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this" (Isaiah 9:7). The people would still walk through the dark for a long time. Hope did not remove the dark. It told them the dark was not the end.

Christians have read this passage as fulfilled in Jesus. Matthew quotes the opening lines when Jesus begins his ministry in Galilee, the very region Isaiah named (Matthew 4:12-17).

Read this week

  • Day 2: Isaiah 40:1-11. Comfort, and a voice in the wilderness.
  • Day 3: Jeremiah 33:14-16. A righteous Branch from David's line.
  • Day 4: Psalm 130. Waiting for the Lord more than watchmen for the morning.
  • Day 5: Lamentations 3:19-26. Hope remembered in the middle of grief.
  • Day 6: Romans 15:4-13. The God of hope, and hope for the nations.
  • Day 7: Matthew 24:36-44. Staying awake for the coming no one can date.

Questions to sit with

  • Isaiah's first hearers waited centuries. What are you waiting for right now, and how are you waiting?
  • What is the difference between hoping something will happen and hoping in someone?
  • Look at the four names in Isaiah 9:6. Which one do you most need this week, and why?
