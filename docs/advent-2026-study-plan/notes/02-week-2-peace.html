Week 2: Peace

Main passage: Isaiah 11:1-10.

Isaiah pictured David's royal family as a tree cut down to a stump, a judgment still ahead of his hearers. Then he promised that a shoot would grow from that stump, a king from Jesse's line, Jesse being David's father. The Spirit of the Lord would rest on him, and he would judge the poor with righteousness.

Then the picture widens. The wolf lives with the lamb, a child plays near the cobra's den, and "the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea" (Isaiah 11:9). Isaiah 11 never uses the word, but it paints what the Old Testament calls shalom, the Hebrew word often behind "peace," which means more than the absence of fighting. It is everything put right: people with God, people with each other, and creation itself.

Christians read the shoot from Jesse as Jesus; Paul quotes Isaiah 11:10 about him (Romans 15:12). The New Testament speaks of peace with God that has already begun through him (Romans 5:1), and of a full peace that is still to come. Readers differ on how literally to take the wolf and the lamb, but the picture itself is clear: this king's peace reaches everything.

Read this week

  • Day 2: Micah 5:2-5. A ruler from Bethlehem who "shall be their peace."
  • Day 3: Luke 1:67-79. Zechariah's song, ending with feet guided into the way of peace.
  • Day 4: Psalm 85. Steadfast love and faithfulness meet; righteousness and peace kiss.
  • Day 5: Romans 5:1-11. Peace with God, given while we were enemies.
  • Day 6: Ephesians 2:13-18. He himself is our peace, the wall broken down.
  • Day 7: John 14:25-27. A peace not as the world gives.

Questions to sit with

  • Shalom means everything put right. Where in your life, or near you, do you most long to see that?
  • Isaiah's king brings peace through justice for the poor (Isaiah 11:4). Why might the two belong together?
  • Ephesians 2:14-16 describes Jews and Gentiles made one people through the cross. Where do you see a wall between people near you, and what small step toward peace might be open to you?
