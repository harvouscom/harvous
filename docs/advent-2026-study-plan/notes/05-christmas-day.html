Christmas: The Word became flesh

Main passages: Luke 2:1-20 and John 1:1-14.

Luke tells what happened. A Roman census sends Joseph and Mary to Bethlehem, the child is born and laid in a manger, and the first people the angels tell are shepherds keeping watch over their flocks at night. An angel announces "good news of great joy that will be for all the people" (Luke 2:10), and then a great company of angels praises God: glory in the highest, and peace on earth.

John tells what it means. Before anything was made, the Word was with God and was God. Then "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" (John 1:14). The hope of week one, the peace of week two, the joy of week three and the love of week four all arrive here, in a person.

Notice how the shepherds respond. They go and see, they tell what they were told, and they return "glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen" (Luke 2:20). Mary, meanwhile, "treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart" (Luke 2:19). Both are good ways to keep Christmas.

Read in the days after

  • Luke 2:21-40. Simeon and Anna meet the child in the temple, after a lifetime of waiting.
  • Matthew 2:1-12. The magi come from the east.
  • Hebrews 1:1-4. God, who spoke through the prophets, has spoken by his Son.
  • Titus 2:11-14. The grace that has appeared, and the hope still to come.

Questions to sit with

  • Of the four weeks, hope, peace, joy and love, which one met you most this Advent?
  • Are you more like the shepherds, who went and told, or like Mary, who pondered? What would it look like to do a little of the other?
  • Simeon and Anna had waited for many years. Advent ends, but the church still waits for Jesus to return. How will you keep waiting well after today?
