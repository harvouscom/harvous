Week 3: Joy

Main passage: Luke 1:39-56.

Mary, newly pregnant, travels to see her relative Elizabeth, who is pregnant too, late in life. When Mary greets her, the child in Elizabeth's womb leaps, and Elizabeth blesses her. Mary answers with a song, often called the Magnificat from its first word in Latin: "My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior" (Luke 1:46-47).

Mary's joy is not the absence of trouble. She is betrothed to Joseph but not yet living with him, and pregnant, in a small town, and her song is full of reversals: the proud scattered, rulers brought down, the humble lifted up, the hungry filled. Her joy rests on what God is doing and on his faithfulness to the promise made to Abraham (Luke 1:54-55). Her song echoes Hannah's prayer, centuries earlier (1 Samuel 2:1-10).

Christians across traditions honor Mary in different ways, and differ on how much to say about her. All of them can read this song as she gives it: praise for what God has done for her, which quickly widens to what he is doing for his people.

Questions to sit with

  • Mary sings of the proud scattered and the hungry filled as if it has already happened, though nothing around her looks different yet. What does that tell you about where her joy comes from?
  • Read the reversals in Luke 1:51-53. Which of them is good news to you, and which is uncomfortable?
  • Psalm 126 holds tears and joy together. How do you hold both this season?
