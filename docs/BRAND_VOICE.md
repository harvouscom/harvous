## Voice Foundation

### Who We Are
We're the friend who gets it. The one who also struggles to remember Bible study insights, who wants deeper understanding without feeling overwhelmed, who values both tradition and helpful innovation. We speak from this place of shared experience, never from expertise or superiority.

### Our Core Philosophy
**"Humans help humans. Tools only help."**

This single principle shapes everything we say. We're not here to revolutionize anyone's spiritual life—we're here to help with the practical problem of remembering Bible study. We're humble about our role and honest about our limitations.

### The Authentic "Why"
Before anything else, remember the genuine heart behind Harvous:

> "I'm making Harvous because I need to remember the Bible, better. The thing is there's so much to Bible study. Humans help humans. Tools only help. Harvous is a tool... Maybe just maybe it'll help others too."

This uncertain, personal, practical honesty is the DNA of our voice.

## Core Voice Attributes

### 1. Personally Grounded
We start with personal need, not market opportunity.

**Why it matters:** Authenticity resonates. When we speak from genuine experience, we connect with others who share the same struggles.

**How it sounds:**
- ✓ "I needed a better way to remember my Bible study thoughts"
- ✗ "Bible study engagement metrics show a clear market opportunity"

**In practice:**
- Lead with "I/we" before "you"
- Share struggles before solutions
- Frame features as personal discoveries
- Maintain founder's voice in key communications

### 2. Conversationally Natural
We write like we talk—with friends, not in boardrooms.

**Why it matters:** Bible study shouldn't feel corporate. Neither should the tools that support it.

**How it sounds:**
- ✓ "The thing is, there's so much to Bible study"
- ✗ "Bible study encompasses multiple learning modalities"

**In practice:**
- Use contractions (it's, we're, you'll)
- Start sentences with "And," "But," "So"
- Include conversational fillers ("the thing is," "you know")
- Use parentheses for asides (like this)
- Keep sentences varied—some long, some short

### 3. Clear and Warm
We're clear about what this is, warm about who it's for.

**Why it matters:** Clarity earns trust. We know exactly what Harvous does and doesn't do — and we say so plainly. Warmth opens the door. We speak like someone who's glad you're here, not like a tool asking to be adopted.

**How it sounds:**
- ✓ "This is a memory tool for your Bible study. That's what it is."
- ✗ "Join thousands who have transformed their Bible study"

**In practice:**
- Say plainly what Harvous is (and what it isn't)
- Use qualifiers only when genuinely uncertain, not as a default posture
- Speak warmly — "I'm glad you're here" energy, not "maybe this will help" hedging
- Celebrate the person, not the product

### 4. Practically Focused
We talk about real benefits, not aspirational promises.

**Why it matters:** People need tools that work, not inspiration that fades.

**How it sounds:**
- ✓ "Save notes right next to verses—they'll be there when you need them"
- ✗ "Transform your spiritual journey with revolutionary note-taking"

**In practice:**
- Describe what happens, not what could happen
- Use concrete verbs (save, find, organize)
- Avoid abstract benefits (transform, revolutionize, optimize)
- Ground features in everyday scenarios

### 5. Opinionated About the Problem
We have a clear point of view.

**Why it matters:** Bible study tools that feel corporate, overwhelming, or cleverly packaged miss the point. We don't pretend otherwise. Knowing what we're not makes what we are more credible.

**How it sounds:**
- ✓ "This isn't a productivity app. It's a memory tool for your Bible study."
- ✗ "Harvous integrates with your existing workflow to enhance scriptural engagement"

**In practice:**
- Take positions, gently but clearly
- Know what you're not — and be willing to say so
- Don't soften the pitch out of politeness; soften it out of honesty
- A sharp "this is what it is" is more welcoming than a vague "this could be anything"

### 6. Warmly Inclusive
We welcome everyone while respecting everyone's unique journey.

**Why it matters:** Bible study looks different for different people. Our tool should honor that diversity.

**How it sounds:**
- ✓ "Whether you're just starting or continuing your journey, there's space for you here"
- ✗ "For serious Bible students ready to take their study to the next level"

**In practice:**
- Use "whether...or" constructions
- Avoid expertise hierarchies
- Describe multiple valid approaches
- Never assume prior knowledge
- Celebrate different paces and styles

## Language Rules

### Rule 1: Positive Language Only
We build up, never tear down. We focus on what we create, not what we avoid.

**Why:** Negativity creates anxiety. Bible study should feel like invitation, not obligation.

**Forbidden words:**
- No, don't, stop, never, can't
- Won't, shouldn't, mustn't
- Fail, wrong, mistake, error
- Difficult, hard, struggle (when describing our tool)

**How to reframe:**

Instead of: "Don't lose your Bible study notes"
Say: "Keep your Bible study thoughts safe and findable"

Instead of: "Never forget important insights"
Say: "Remember what matters most to you"

Instead of: "Stop using scattered notes"
Say: "Bring your thoughts together in one place"

### Rule 2: Avoid "Insights"
We use simpler, more natural words for what people capture during Bible study.

**Why:** "Insights" sounds corporate and elevated. People have "thoughts," "notes," "ideas," "reflections"—language that feels personal and accessible.

**Use instead:**
- Thoughts
- Notes
- Ideas
- Reflections
- Discoveries
- Understanding
- What you're learning

**Exception:** When quoting academic sources or in technical documentation where precision requires it.

### Rule 3: Use Parentheses for Authenticity
Parenthetical asides add conversational warmth and humility.

**Why:** They create intimacy, like we're sharing an aside just with you.

**How to use them:**
- For humble qualifications: "We think (hope?) this will help"
- For friendly additions: "Your notes stay private (unless you choose to share)"
- For conversational asides: "Threads organize your thoughts (think of them like collections that go on a journey)"

**Don't overuse:** One per paragraph maximum. They lose impact when overused.

### Rule 4: Simplify Relentlessly
When choosing between simple and sophisticated, choose simple.

**Why:** Complexity creates barriers. Simplicity creates welcome.

**Test every sentence:**
- Could a high schooler understand this?
- Would I say this out loud to a friend?
- Does this use the smallest words possible?

**Examples:**

Instead of: "Leverage our proprietary organizational framework"
Say: "Organize your thoughts your way"

Instead of: "Optimize your scriptural engagement"
Say: "Get more from your Bible reading"

Instead of: "Utilize advanced cross-referencing capabilities"
Say: "Connect related verses easily"

## Tone Spectrum

Our tone shifts slightly based on context, but always stays within our brand territory.

### Everyday Tone (Default)
**Use for:** Most content, feature descriptions, general communication

**Characteristics:**
- Friendly and approachable
- Clear and straightforward
- Gently encouraging
- Humble confidence

**Example:**
"Save any thought that comes to you while reading. A question, a connection, something you want to remember—it all has a place here. We'll help you find it later when you need it."

### Welcoming Tone
**Use for:** Onboarding, first-time user communication, invitations

**Characteristics:**
- Extra warmth
- Explicit inclusion
- Patient guidance
- No assumptions

**Example:**
"Welcome! Whether this is your first time opening a Bible or you've been studying for years, you're in the right place. Start wherever feels comfortable—there's no wrong way to begin."

### Celebratory Tone
**Use for:** Milestones, achievements, community wins

**Characteristics:**
- Genuine excitement
- Personal focus
- Specific recognition
- Humble joy

**Example:**
"Look at this! You've collected thoughts on this theme for three months now. Reading back through, can you see how your understanding has grown? We love seeing journeys like this."

### Helpful Tone
**Use for:** Instructions, problem-solving, feature explanations

**Characteristics:**
- Clear and direct
- Patient guidance
- Practical focus
- Encouraging

**Example:**
"Looking for a specific note? Start typing what you remember—even just a word or two. We'll search through all your threads and show you where it lives."

### Reflective Tone
**Use for:** About page, manifesto, vision content

**Characteristics:**
- Thoughtful and considered
- Honest vulnerability
- Deeper purpose
- Personal conviction

**Example:**
"We built Harvous because we kept losing the moments that mattered. Those Sunday morning discoveries, those mid-week connections—they'd slip away before we could hold onto them. We thought: what if they didn't have to?"

## Writing Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Personal Opening
Start with "I/we" before moving to "you."

**Structure:**
1. Personal experience or struggle
2. Realization or question
3. What we built
4. How it helps you

**Example:**
"I used to write notes all over my Bible, in journals, on my phone—anywhere I could grab in the moment. Then I'd never find them again. So we built threads: one place for all those thoughts, organized by what they're about instead of when you wrote them. Now when you need to find something, it's right there."

### Pattern 2: The Simple Explanation
Break down complexity into everyday language.

**Structure:**
1. Simple statement of what it is
2. Familiar comparison
3. Specific benefit
4. Invitation to try

**Example:**
"Threads are like collections of related notes. Think of them as topics you're exploring—maybe a character study, a sermon series, or a question you're wrestling with. As you add thoughts to a thread, you build understanding over time. Start one for whatever you're studying right now."

### Pattern 3: The Honest Qualifier
Acknowledge limitations while highlighting value.

**Structure:**
1. What it does well
2. What it doesn't do
3. Why that's okay
4. Invitation anyway

**Example:**
"Harvous helps you remember your Bible study thoughts. It won't write your notes for you or tell you what to think. That's your journey. But it will keep everything safe, organized, and findable. And honestly, that's what we needed most."

### Pattern 4: The Shared Struggle
Connect through common challenges.

**Structure:**
1. Common experience
2. Validation
3. Our approach
4. Your benefit

**Example:**
"Ever have an 'aha moment' during Bible reading, then completely forget it by the next day? We've been there. That's why we made it easy to capture thoughts in the moment—just a few taps and it's saved. Your future self will thank you."

### Pattern 5: The Parenthetical Aside
Add warmth through conversational asides.

**Structure:**
1. Main point
2. (Friendly aside or clarification)
3. Continue main point

**Example:**
"Your notes stay completely private (unless you choose to share with a study group). We think Bible study is personal—you should control who sees what you're learning."

### Pattern 6: The Founder Close
End key communications — about pages, launch emails, major announcements — with a personal sign-off. Not a button. A person.

**Structure:**
1. Simple, warm close
2. Genuine invitation to respond
3. Real name (and email when appropriate)

**Example:**
"That's what we're building. If you're trying to do something similar, I'd love to hear from you.
— Derek"

**Why it works:** The 37signals move. Jason Fried puts his email in the copy. It signals: there's a real person here who cares, and they're reachable. That's the energy.

## Channel-Specific Guidelines

### Website Copy

**Tone:** Everyday with welcoming undertones
**Length:** Concise—people scan
**Structure:** Clear headlines, short paragraphs, natural flow

**Do:**
- Use descriptive headlines that complete thoughts
- Keep paragraphs to 2-3 sentences
- Include white space for breathing room
- End sections with clear next steps

**Example:**

**Headline:** "Your Bible study thoughts, all in one place"

**Body:** "Notes from sermons. Thoughts while reading. Questions you're wrestling with. They all belong somewhere you can find them later.

Harvous brings them together in threads—simple collections organized by what they're about. One note can live in multiple threads, because understanding connects across topics.

Start capturing what matters to you."

### Email Communication

**Tone:** Personal and conversational
**Length:** Shorter than you think—respect inbox time
**Structure:** Friendly greeting, one clear point, simple close

**Do:**
- Write like a friend updating a friend
- One main idea per email
- Personal sign-offs from real people
- Clear subject lines that sound human

**Example:**

**Subject:** "A small update about threads"

**Body:**
"Hey,

Quick note: we just made it easier to move notes between threads. Sometimes you realize a thought belongs somewhere else, and that was harder than it should've been.

Now it's simple—just drag and drop.

Hope this helps,
Derek"

### Social Media

**Tone:** Warm and engaging
**Length:** Platform appropriate
**Structure:** Hook, insight, invitation

**Do:**
- Share genuine discoveries
- Ask real questions
- Celebrate user wins (with permission)
- Use conversational language
- Include relevant hashtags naturally

**Example (X/Twitter):**
"Been thinking about how we organize Bible study thoughts. Collection-only filing feels wrong—like forcing connections into boxes. Threads feel right—like letting connections grow naturally.

Anyone else feel this way? What's your system?"

**Example (Instagram):**
[Image: Person with coffee and Bible]

"Sunday mornings hit different when you know your thoughts won't disappear by Monday.

That's why we built Harvous—to give your Bible study somewhere to stay.

What's one thing you discovered this week that you want to remember?"

### In-App Copy

**Tone:** Helpful and encouraging
**Length:** Ultra-concise—people are mid-task
**Structure:** Clear action or benefit stated simply

**Do:**
- Guide without explaining everything
- Celebrate small actions
- Make errors friendly
- Encourage next steps naturally

**Examples:**

**Empty state:**
"No threads yet. Start one for whatever you're studying right now."

**First note:**
"Your first thought! Add more as they come to you."

**Search empty state:**
"We'll search through all your notes and threads. What are you looking for?"

**Error message:**
"Hmm, something didn't save. Want to try again?"

**Success message:**
"Saved to your thread. It'll be here when you need it."

### Feature Announcements

**Tone:** Excited but not over-hyped
**Length:** Enough to explain clearly
**Structure:** Problem → solution → how it helps → try it

**Example:**

**New Feature: Note Connections**

"You know how sometimes a note belongs in multiple threads? Like when a verse speaks to both your prayer study and your character study?

We've been thinking about this. One note, multiple homes—because understanding doesn't live in just one category.

Now you can add any note to as many threads as make sense. Your thought, your way of organizing it.

Try it next time you're capturing something that connects across topics. We think you'll like it."

### Documentation/Help

**Tone:** Clear and patient
**Length:** As long as needed, no longer
**Structure:** Overview → steps → examples → what's next

**Do:**
- Assume no prior knowledge
- Use "you" and "we" liberally
- Show, don't just tell
- Link to related help articles
- End with encouragement

**Example:**

**Creating Your First Thread**

Threads help you organize related Bible study thoughts. Think of them like topics you're exploring over time.

Here's how to create one:

1. Tap the "+" button
2. Choose "New Thread"
3. Give it a name (something like "Psalms study" or "Prayer in James")
4. Start adding notes

As you study, add thoughts to this thread. Over time, you'll build up understanding on this topic.

Many people start with one thread per book they're studying. Others organize by theme. Both work great—choose what feels right for you.

Ready to try? Create a thread for whatever you're studying right now."

## Quick Reference

### Voice Checklist
Before publishing any content, verify:

- [ ] Does it sound like talking to a friend?
- [ ] Would the founder say this?
- [ ] Is it positive (no "don't," "never," "can't")?
- [ ] Did I avoid "insights" and use simpler words?
- [ ] Is it clear about what this is, warm about who it's for?
- [ ] Does it start with personal experience?
- [ ] Are benefits practical, not aspirational?
- [ ] Is it inclusive of different journeys?
- [ ] Would a high schooler understand it?
- [ ] Does it feel authentic, not polished?

## The Heart of It All

At the end of the day, Harvous voice is about honesty. We're building this tool because we need it. We hope it helps others. We're learning as we go.

Every word should feel like it comes from that place—genuine, humble, helpful.

When in doubt, ask: "Would I say this to a friend over coffee?"

If not, rewrite it until you would.

"Humans help humans. Tools only help."
