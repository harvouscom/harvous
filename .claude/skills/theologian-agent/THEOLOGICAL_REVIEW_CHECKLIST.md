# Theological review checklist

Use this when reviewing **Harvous-authored** thread titles, note bodies, subtitles, onboarding copy, or marketing that explains or applies Scripture. For **UI or code** that powers references, use **scripture-agent**.

---

## A. Scripture and claims

- [ ] **Citations match claims**: Each major claim tied to a passage fits that passage’s immediate context (who is speaking, audience, genre). Flag proof-texting or “this verse means X” when X is not supportable from the verse or its paragraph/chapter.
- [ ] **No overreach**: Distinguish **text** vs **interpretation** vs **application**. Flag language that presents one reading as the only faithful one when Christians in good faith disagree.
- [ ] **Tradition vs Bible**: Do not attribute to “the Bible” what is purely tradition, popular teaching, or one stream’s emphasis unless framed honestly (“many Christians have read this as…”, “one helpful lens is…”).
- [ ] **Numbers and facts**: Historical or narrative details (names, events, order) match the text; no invented scenes presented as Scripture.

---

## B. Tone and epistemology (Harvous voice)

- [ ] **Humble confidence**: Clear where the text is clear; honest where scholars or traditions disagree. Flag “obviously,” “the Bible proves,” “God wants you to know today that…,” or similar certainty theater.
- [ ] **No hype words** (per content writing guide): revolutionary, transform, unlock, insights, ultimate, etc., when applied to spiritual outcomes.
- [ ] **Peer, not pulpit**: Sounds like a thoughtful friend, not a scold or a lecturer grading the reader’s faith.
- [ ] **Inclusive warmth**: New readers and long-time readers can both enter without being shamed for pace or background.

---

## C. Gospel shape and proportion (when teaching redemptive narrative or ethics)

- [ ] **Grace and effort**: Works-righteousness or anxiety-inducing checklists framed as “what God requires to accept you” → flag as **must-fix** or **should-fix** depending on severity. Prefer rest in Christ plus proportionate response, in line with the passage.
- [ ] **Law and gospel**: Heavy demand without good news in view (where the passage is not solely law)—note **should-fix** and suggest balance without forcing a single denominational formula.
- [ ] **Christ’s place**: In NT exposition especially, the piece does not sideline Jesus as mediator and Lord unless the content is intentionally OT-only and says so.

---

## D. Light orthodoxy guardrails

Apply only to copy **stated as Harvous’s own teaching**, not block quotes or “some believe…” reporting.

- [ ] **Creeds broadly shared**: Flag flat contradiction of widely held creedal claims (e.g. denial of God’s triune identity, denial of Christ’s resurrection, Scripture as irrelevant to faith) if asserted as true teaching.
- [ ] **Denominational fine points**: Do not treat one tradition’s disputed point as Harvous doctrine unless the product explicitly targets that tradition. Prefer “Christians disagree here; worth discussing with others you trust.”

---

## E. Sensitive and pastoral topics

- [ ] **Mental health, trauma, abuse**: Study copy must not imply prayer or Bible study alone replaces professional care or safety planning. Flag shaming or “just trust more” as fix for clinical or crisis situations.
- [ ] **Prosperity or fear**: Flag guarantees of health/wealth/status from faith, or manipulative fear of divine punishment for normal human limits.
- [ ] **Politics**: Flag copy that equates a party, nation, or leader with God’s kingdom in a way that reads as Harvous’s official stance.

---

## F. Separation from scripture-agent

- [ ] If the problem is **wrong reference**, **missing translation**, **pill markup**, or **server-side verse fetch** → note “scripture-agent / data-agent” and do not pretend this checklist fixes tooling.

---

## After the checklist

Summarize findings using the **Must-fix / Should-fix / Suggestion** severities from `SKILL.md`. When in doubt between severities, prefer **should-fix** and explain uncertainty in **Notes**.
