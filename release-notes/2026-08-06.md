# What's New in Harvous — August 6, 2026

**Release Date:** August 6, 2026
**Versions:** v2.21.0–v2.33.0

A big day for churches. Your church gets a Planner it can actually work in, a shelf for what it teaches from, and clearer lines between who does what. There is one thing here for everybody: a Resource Library of your own.

---

## Updates in This Release

### The Planner, at full size

**What changed:**
- The Planner opens across the whole pane instead of the sidebar column
- Three ways to look at the same plan: a board you drag weeks around on, a calendar, and a list
- The sermon editor is a rail down the side now, not a window over the top
- An idea with no date yet sits in a strip under the plan, waiting for one

**How it helps you:**
- You can see the quarter you are planning while you edit a week of it
- Dragging a sermon to another Sunday is one motion, rather than opening it, changing the date and saving
- The idea you had on Tuesday is in the plan instead of in your head

### A series knows how long it runs

**What changed:**
- A series used to be a label typed onto each week. It is now a thing you can open, rename, and see the whole run of
- You set how many weeks a series runs, and the weeks you have not written yet appear as empty slots
- A series tells you how many of its weeks are still to write
- An unwritten week is marked the same way everywhere it appears, in the board, the calendar and the list
- Renaming a series renames it everywhere it appears

**How it helps you:**
- A six-week series looks like six weeks from the day you plan it, not like one week that keeps growing
- You can see at a glance what is left to write, instead of counting
- Renaming a series mid-run no longer quietly splits it in two
- Planning a quarter takes one pass, and if a week is already taken it stops there and tells you which one, rather than skipping it silently

### A shelf for your church

**What changed:**
- Your church can keep the links and files it teaches from in one place
- Staff choose who each resource is for: everyone in the church, or leaders only
- A resource can belong to the whole church, or to particular rooms, like Youth or the Tuesday study
- Anything on the shelf can be attached to a planned sermon or gathering, from either size of the editor

**How it helps you:**
- The commentary your pastor keeps recommending is somewhere you can actually find it
- Leaders' notes and answer keys stay with leaders, without a second place to keep them
- When you open this Sunday's sermon, what it draws on is already sitting there
- If your church's plan lapses, the shelf stays readable. You do not lose what you have been given

### A suggestion box

**What changed:**
- Anyone connected to your church can suggest a resource for the shelf
- A pastor or admin reviews suggestions and decides what gets added
- Suggestions are the one thing your church sees your name on

**How it helps you:**
- The talk that helped you can reach the rest of your church
- Your church's shelf is shaped by the people using it, not only by staff
- Everything else stays private, as it was. Harvous does not tell your church what you read, what you wrote, or whether you opened anything at all. A suggestion is different because you chose to send it, and a pastor cannot reply to it without knowing who it came from

### A Resource Library of your own

**What changed:**
- Keep links and files in one place, alongside your notes
- This one is for everybody, not only churches

**How it helps you:**
- The article you meant to come back to has somewhere to live
- Your handouts and PDFs sit with the study they belong to, instead of in a downloads folder

### Your church's own clock

**What changed:**
- A church sets its time zone and lists the services it actually holds, like 9:00 and 10:45 on Sunday
- Service times show as the church's wall clock, never converted to yours

**How it helps you:**
- "This Sunday, 9:00 AM" means what your church means by it, even if you are reading three time zones away
- A church with a morning and an evening service can plan both, instead of choosing one

### A plan for each ministry

**What changed:**
- A ministry channel or church shared space can carry its own teaching plan
- A channel plans content now, rather than meetings. Its Planner asks for studies and devotionals instead of a gathering time
- Youth's Wednesday plan sits alongside the church's Sunday plan rather than competing with it

**How it helps you:**
- The person who runs a ministry can plan it without touching the main service
- A channel that publishes weekly is not pretending to be a room that meets
- What you see on Home is unchanged: your church's next service, and nothing else

### Volunteers can lead a space

**What changed:**
- Someone connected to your church can be given leadership of one space, from that space's own people list
- Leadership is granted deliberately and can be taken back the same way

**How it helps you:**
- The volunteer who actually runs the youth channel can publish to it, without being added to church staff
- It stays narrow on purpose: one space, and nothing else in the church

### Engagement, in counts only

**What changed:**
- A new Engagement pane shows how many people are connected to your church, and how many follow each channel

**How it helps you:**
- You can see whether a ministry is reaching people
- Harvous never shows a church who wrote what, or whether anyone wrote at all. Your notes are yours, and this pane does not change that

### Pastor and Teacher are different jobs

**What changed:**
- A pastor sets what the church teaches and what notes start from. A teacher reads the plan they teach from and publishes to channels
- The two roles previously granted exactly the same thing

**How it helps you:**
- A church can hand out the right amount of access instead of all of it

### Polish

**What changed:**
- Every pane under My Church shows a loading indicator while it is fetching, and the Planner opens noticeably faster
- Empty panes say what belongs there and offer the way out, rather than showing a bare line of text
- The board's future weeks show their dates, and the week you are in stands out instead of the ones you are not
- Calendar entries are sized for a day, and the sidebar opens and closes faster
- Menu rows are bigger, which matters most on a phone
- The sermon editor's Save and Delete stay at the bottom of the rail instead of drifting up the page

**How it helps you:**
- You can tell the difference between "still loading" and "there is nothing here"
- A pane you have not filled yet tells you what it is for
- The plan reads at a glance instead of asking you to work out which week is which
