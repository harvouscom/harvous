# What's New in Harvous — September 5, 2026

**Release Date:** September 5, 2026
**Versions:** v3.3.0 - v3.5.2

Harvous Plus costs less, and the pricing has one fewer thing to explain. Reminders can arrive on Sunday morning and midweek carrying the day's verse, a shared space can decide what it studies next, and adding Harvous to your home screen is one tap on Android.

---

## Updates in This Release

### Harvous Plus is now $6 a month, or $36 a year

**What changed:**
- Plus moves from $7/mo and $49/yr to $6/mo and $36/yr.
- The founding offer — a discounted first year for the first 99 people — has been retired rather than moved onto the new price.

**How it helps you:**
- A year of Plus is now exactly half of twelve months of monthly, so the annual price makes its own case without a countdown or a limited number of places.
- Nothing renews at a different number than you bought it at. The price you see is the price it stays.
- If you are already on an older price, you keep it. Nothing about your existing subscription changes.

### Clearer words when checkout cannot start

**What changed:**
- When something goes wrong starting a checkout, Harvous now says so in its own words, and says whose problem it is.
- Previously the payment provider's internal wording could reach the page — including a message about an invalid token, which described a credential of ours, not anything belonging to the person reading it.

**How it helps you:**
- An error on a payment page is worrying enough without it appearing to be about your account.
- Where the fault is ours, the message now says so, and says nothing was charged.

### The Plus page lists only what Plus can do

**What changed:**
- Challenges has been removed from the list of what Plus includes. It is built, but switched off for everyone while it is redesigned, so it should not have been advertised.

**How it helps you:**
- The list of what you are paying for is now a list of things you can actually use.

### Opening a verse in the Bible reader works from any note

**What changed:**
- Expanding a passage card into the Bible reader did nothing when the note was still unsaved, which is every note started from a verse, from Today's passage, or from a suggestion.
- The same button did nothing at all without an account.
- Starting a new note immediately after typing could make the editor disappear.

**How it helps you:**
- The chapter opens from wherever the passage was mentioned, and the note is one tap away the whole time you are reading.

### Every chip on Home goes somewhere

**What changed:**
- The season pill under the greeting looked pressable and was not. It is a label now.
- Today's Passage sat between four pressable chips as plain text; it is a chip.
- The chips naming a theme or a pair of passages you keep returning to now open a search for it when there is no grouping to review yet.

**How it helps you:**
- Nothing in the sentence at the top of Home looks like a link and then refuses to be one.

### The Note button starts a new note when you are already writing

**What changed:**
- Pressing Note while on a note used to do nothing, because it was trying to take you back to the note you were already looking at.

**How it helps you:**
- One control both returns to what you were writing and starts the next thing, depending on where you are.

### Saving a template no longer follows you into the next note

**What changed:**
- After making a template, every note started afterwards claimed to be a template too, until the tab was closed.
- A template saved with a chosen colour showed a different, arbitrary colour on the note that used it.

**How it helps you:**
- A template is finished when you save it, and the colour you picked is the colour you see.

### Trying Harvous without an account behaves like the real thing

**What changed:**
- A note written on a verse the first time round was being discarded rather than kept.
- Deleting a note, changing the Bible translation, and the keyboard shortcuts for a new note, Activity and the reader all work on the device now.
- Where something genuinely needs an account, Harvous says so and offers one, instead of failing quietly or showing an error.

**How it helps you:**
- What you write while trying Harvous is still there when you make an account, and nothing you can press is a dead end.

### The font setting says what it changes

**What changed:**
- In Settings under Appearance, the third option beside Colors and Imagery was labelled "Type". It is now "Scripture font".
- It sets the face Scripture is shown in — in the Bible reader, and on the verses that come back in Review.

**How it helps you:**
- "Type" sat between two background choices, where it read as a kind of background rather than a choice of lettering.
- The name now says both what the setting changes and where you will see it.

### A reminder on Sunday and midweek

**What changed:**
- Harvous can send you two reminders a week: one on Sunday morning before church, carrying the day's verse, and one midweek on a day you pick, carrying the chapter you left off in. Tapping one opens straight to it.
- You turn them on under Settings, Reminders. Your browser asks permission once.
- They work in desktop browsers and in installed web apps. On an iPhone they work once Harvous is on your Home Screen, which is the only way Apple allows a web app to notify you at all.
- They stay quiet on purpose: never more than two a week, never on a day you have already opened Harvous, and they pause themselves if they go unopened for a few weeks. Changing anything in the settings starts them again.
- Along the way: notification titles fit on one line, a tap opens the right screen even when the app was not already running, and a test send that fails now tells you why.

**How it helps you:**
- The reminder carries the thing itself rather than asking you to come back and find it.
- If they stop being useful to you, they stop on their own. You do not have to remember to switch them off.

### Deciding what a shared space studies next

**What changed:**
- Anyone in a shared space can suggest what the room studies next, and whoever runs the room picks. It stays off until a room turns it on. The full story is in [What's next](launches/shared-space-study-suggestions.md) — this is the day's own record.
- Other things a member of a room can now reach: you can mark a study plan finished for yourself without closing the run for everyone, and closing the run does not claim you finished it. A group with no church behind it can have a co-leader. A room that gathers shows who else is here. You can answer someone's annotation from the note itself, instead of hunting for the exact words they highlighted. The room's note templates sit in its Tools, and the Coming up card can open the passage in the reader rather than only starting a note about it.

**How it helps you:**
- Your progress through a plan is yours. Finishing it is a thing you say about your own study, not a thing that ends the study for the room.

### Adding Harvous to your home screen

**What changed:**
- On Android, Chrome can add Harvous for you now. The card on Home has an Install button that opens the browser's own install prompt, so there are no written steps to follow.
- Where a browser cannot do it for you, the steps have been rebuilt. You pick iPhone or Android, and each step shows the control you are hunting for — the Share box on iPhone, the three dot menu in Chrome — beside the browser's own icon.
- The instructions used to say that only Safari can add to the home screen on an iPhone. That stopped being true in iOS 16.4, and the wording is corrected.
- There were two sets of instructions inside Harvous that disagreed with each other. There is one now.
- The whole card on Home responds to a tap, not only the line at the bottom of it.

**How it helps you:**
- On Android it is one tap, and you never read a step.
- On iPhone the steps match what is actually on your screen, and they no longer tell you a browser cannot do something it can.
- Harvous then opens from your home screen in its own window, with no browser bar around it.
