# What's New in Harvous — August 14, 2026

**Release Date:** August 14, 2026
**Version:** v2.59.1

---

## Updates in This Release

### Read the Bible in Harvous

**What changed:**
- Open any chapter as its own page, the way a note opens. Move between chapters, switch translation, and pick the text size that suits you.
- Choose verses rather than dragging across characters. A small menu appears with everything you can do with what you picked.
- Highlight in five colours, write an annotation, look at related passages, or start a note from the verses you chose.
- Names and places in the text carry a faint dotted underline. Tap one to read who or where it is without leaving the chapter.

**How it helps you:**
- You can open Harvous to read, not only when you have something to write down.

### See where you have already written

**What changed:**
- A slim mark in the left margin shows which verses your notes cover. Its length is the passage — a single verse is a short tick, a longer reading is a longer line, and two notes on the same verses sit side by side.
- Point at one and that passage lifts onto a card while the rest of the chapter softens, so you can see exactly what the note is about. The card names the note, and opens it at that passage.
- Turn the marks off from the reader's details panel under Margin notes if you would rather read without them.

**How it helps you:**
- A chapter you have studied before says so, without you having to remember.

### Move around the Bible freely

**What changed:**
- Tap the chapter title to jump to any book or chapter — chapters are laid out as a grid of numbers, so picking one is a glance rather than a scroll.
- Reading carries on past the end of a book — Exodus 40 now leads to Leviticus 1 instead of stopping.

**How it helps you:**
- You can go wherever you are headed without leaving the page you are reading.

### Chapters open straight away

**What changed:**
- Moving to the next or previous chapter no longer waits — the next chapter is ready before you ask for it, including across the end of a book.
- Short waits no longer flash a "loading" line at you.

### Your highlights are in one place now

**What changed:**
- A highlight you make while reading and a highlight you make inside a note are the same highlight. Mark Exodus 5:1 in either place and it is there in both.

**How it helps you:**
- Nothing is stranded in the surface you happened to be using at the time.

### Choose how Scripture is set

**What changed:**
- Four reading typefaces in Settings — Sans, Serif, Hand, and OpenDyslexic, a face designed to be easier to read with dyslexia.
- Try a different face for one sitting from the reader's details panel, without changing your default.

**How it helps you:**
- Long reading is easier when the type suits your eyes.

### A tidier home sidebar

**What changed:**
- Cards are gathered under three headings — Continue, Following, and Suggested — instead of sitting in one long stack where every card announced itself separately.

**How it helps you:**
- You can see at a glance what you were in the middle of, what someone else has put in front of you, and what is simply worth a look.

### Fixes

**What changed:**
- Typing "Philemon" no longer turns into "Philippians".
- Save confirmations read cleanly again instead of ending mid-punctuation.
- The stacked suggestion cards no longer show a pale band along the front card's edge.
- Going back from a folder or a thread now uses the same button as everywhere else, at a size worth aiming for.
- The grip for reordering your spaces moved to the right, matching how you reorder everything else — and a space's icon no longer disappears when you point at it.
- A long line of card detail is trimmed to one line rather than spilling over three.

---

## Tips for Using New Features

- **Select more than one verse:** Hold Shift and choose a second verse to take in a whole passage at once.
- **Change a highlight's colour:** Highlight first, then pick from the colours that appear — you will see each one on the verse as you try it.
- **Set your reading face:** Settings, then Appearance, then Type.
