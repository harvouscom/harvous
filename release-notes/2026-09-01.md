# What's New in Harvous — September 1, 2026

**Release Date:** September 1, 2026
**Versions:** v2.128.0 - v2.128.1

Two new parts of Harvous Plus: Review, which brings your own notes back to you on a schedule, and Challenges, short guided paths through study you have already written.

---

## Updates in This Release

### Review

**What changed:**
- You can add a note, a highlight, a verse, a connection between two notes, or a whole Thread to Review.
- Harvous asks you about it later, one question at a time, on a small card at the bottom of the screen. It sits beside your study rather than taking you away from it, and it stays with you as you move between your notes, the Bible and your Activity view.
- You write what you remember, or say you have it in mind, and then Harvous opens your actual note. The question moves to the top of it, so you can read what you wrote and answer in the same place.
- You say whether you recalled it or almost had it, and Harvous decides when to ask again: tomorrow if you needed to look, four days if you almost had it, two weeks if you knew it. After three clean recalls in a row the gaps start widening on their own.
- A Review section on your Activity view holds up to three things worth returning to. Never more, and never a count of what it is not showing.
- Not now, Pause, and Remove from Review are all one tap away on every row.
- If you are new to it, Harvous can pick three notes to start with. It looks for study that mattered to you and that you have not opened in a while, rather than the notes you wrote yesterday.

**How it helps you:**
- The notes you spent time on come back before you have forgotten them, instead of sinking to the bottom of a list.
- Answering never costs you your place. The question follows you, and the note it asks about opens over whatever you were doing, with a way straight back.
- The schedule is a plain rule you can predict, not a hidden algorithm. Harvous can always tell you when something is coming back.
- Nothing is graded. What you write is yours, kept alongside the note, and never marked right or wrong.
- Every question is written by hand and filled in with your own material. Harvous does not generate answers, interpretations, or study content for you.

### Challenges

**What changed:**
- Start a short path from something you already have: a Thread you have been building, a note titled as a question, a verse you want to keep, or a connection you made between two notes.
- Each path is four or five steps. Recall the central question first, add the evidence in the text, link one more passage and say why it belongs, name what is still unresolved, and write a summary for your future self.
- Every step leaves something you own. A link step makes a real connection, and a summary step becomes a real note in your library.
- Skip sits beside Done on every step. A path where you skipped a step still finishes, and nothing calls it a miss.
- On your Activity view, Harvous may offer to strengthen a Thread once it has enough notes in it.

**How it helps you:**
- A Thread that had been growing without a shape gets one, in about the time it takes to read a chapter.
- You end up with more of your own writing than you started with, rather than a checklist you ticked.
- There is no scoring, no leaderboard, and no streak. A path is something to walk when it helps.

### Smaller things

**What changed:**
- Review and Challenges are now listed as part of Harvous Plus rather than as coming soon.
- Deleting a note removes the questions Harvous was going to ask about it. A challenge built on that note is retired instead of deleted, so the notes it helped you write stay where they are.

**How it helps you:**
- Nothing is left behind asking you about study you no longer have.

---

## Tips for Using These Features

- Look for the Review section on your Activity view. If nothing is waiting, it stays out of the way entirely.
- The Review card can be folded down to a single line while you read, and opened again when you are ready.
- Add a note to Review from the note's own menu, next to Share and Pin.
- Try writing something before you reveal your note, even a fragment. The attempt is the part that makes it stick.
- A note titled with a question mark can start a path of its own.
