# What's New in Harvous — September 4, 2026

**Release Date:** September 4, 2026
**Versions:** v2.158.3 - v2.166.6

Review learns to ask about the chapters you read, and starts telling you what it just asked you. Plus a pass over the words it uses, so it sounds like a person rather than a system.

---

## Updates in This Release

### Chapters you read become questions

**What changed:**
- Review can now ask about a chapter you have read, not only about a verse or a note you wrote. It asks which verse is in it, invites you to finish one, has you put three verses in the order they come, or asks who appears in it.
- Only chapters you actually read count. A glance at a chapter is not reading it, however many times you glance, and a chapter is never asked about on the day you read it.
- Every answer comes from the chapter's own words or from a reference index. Harvous does not ask what a chapter is about, because a chapter of the Bible is about many things and that is not a question with an answer.

**How it helps you:**
- Reading now leads somewhere. Until today, the time you spent in the Bible reader left no trace anywhere else in Harvous.
- The questions are about what is on the page rather than about your interpretation of it.

### Write about what you just read

**What changed:**
- Your Activity view can now offer to start a note about a chapter you read today or yesterday, with the passage already in it and one short question to answer.
- Where you highlighted a verse in that chapter, that verse is quoted in the note, ready for you to write underneath.
- The offer steps aside once Review has taken that chapter up.

**How it helps you:**
- The moment to write about a chapter is while it is still fresh, and that moment used to pass without anything asking.
- It only ever quotes a verse you chose yourself.

### Review tells you what it asked

**What changed:**
- After you answer, the card now shows the question, then what you said with the parts that landed marked, then the passage or the right answer.
- Before, it showed only an answer, with nothing above it saying what had been asked.
- Where a question deliberately hid what it was about — say where this is from, pick the note this line is from — the answer now names it, because the reason for hiding it is over.

**How it helps you:**
- A question you got wrong is worth sitting with, and you can only do that if you can see what it was.
- You can tell which words of yours landed, rather than only whether the whole answer did.

### Plainer words

**What changed:**
- Where Harvous used to say a passage was "Still fragile", "Forming" or "Holding", it now says you are still learning it, you know it, or it is slipping away, on a small marker beside the passage rather than as a word at the end of a line.
- "Fill in the blanks" is what that exercise is called everywhere now. It used to have three different names depending on where you met it.
- "Step back a rung" is "Make it easier". Nothing on screen ever showed you a ladder.

**How it helps you:**
- Nothing on a Review row asks you to learn a vocabulary first.

### When nothing is waiting

**What changed:**
- An empty Review card now says whether you are up to date and when the next one comes back — later today, tomorrow, on Tuesday — rather than a single line saying nothing is waiting.
- Things scheduled for later, and things you paused or put down, are now folded away under the Review section on your Activity view.
- The separate Review page is gone. Everything it held lives on Activity now, including picking up something you set aside, which used to be possible only on that page.

**How it helps you:**
- You can see your whole queue without leaving the page you started on.
- Pausing or removing something is no longer a decision you cannot undo.

### Trying Review without Plus

**What changed:**
- The sample question now changes from day to day rather than showing the same verse every morning.
- Hiding the Plus row no longer takes the sample question away with it.
- The offer arrives after you have answered, alongside what you just did, with a plain way to say not now.

**How it helps you:**
- You get to try the thing before being asked about it.

### Smaller things

**What changed:**
- Review's answer options now fit on a phone. Long verse openings used to run off both edges of the card and take the letter with them.
- The list of things you have shared is half the height it was, and no longer shows the raw link for every item.
- Only your own highlights feed the suggestions on your Activity view.

**How it helps you:**
- Answering on a phone works the way it does on a laptop.
- Your shared links are a list you can read down rather than a block to pick through.

---

## Tips for Using These Features

- Open a chapter in the Bible reader and stay a while. That is all it takes for Review to have something to ask you later.
- On an empty Review card, the line about when the next one returns is a real date. You can close Harvous until then.
- The fold under the Review section holds everything scheduled and everything you set aside. It is the way back from a pause.

## Also in This Release

A batch about shared spaces, and mostly about things the app had already decided but never showed you. Several of these were working on the server the whole time with no way to reach them from a screen. The planner also stops using three different words for one button. Plus one from search: every result now carries a mark saying what it is.

### Your own way through a study plan

**What changed:**
- A plan you are walking shows how far along you are, and you can mark it finished
- This is separate from a leader closing the run for the group — closing the study does not finish it for you, and finishing it yourself does not close it for anyone else
- A personal reading plan can be finished too, not only one shared with a group

**How it helps you:**
- Where you are in a study is a fact about your own reading, so it no longer waits on somebody else to declare the group done
- A plan you finished can leave your home screen instead of sitting there indefinitely

### A second leader in any group

**What changed:**
- The owner of a shared space can make someone a co-leader, in any group
- Until now this only worked in rooms connected to a church, so an ordinary study group had exactly one person who could run it
- A co-leader cannot make further co-leaders; that stays with the person who owns the room

**How it helps you:**
- Whoever else keeps your group running can pin the study, start a Thread and plan a gathering, rather than waiting on one person
- If you are away, the group is not stuck

### Answering someone on a passage

**What changed:**
- You can reply to another person's highlight directly from it
- Before, answering someone meant finding and re-selecting the exact words they had marked
- A conversation on a passage now reads oldest to newest, the way it happened

**How it helps you:**
- Responding to what someone saw in a verse is one tap from their note about it
- Following an exchange no longer means reading it backwards

### The room you are standing in

**What changed:**
- A shared space shows who else is there
- The passage on the space's "showing up in your notes" card can be set aside with "Not now", and moves to the next passage your group has cited most
- Setting one aside follows you between your devices, and stays inside that room — resting a passage in one group does not hide it in another, or at home
- "Coming up" offers to open the passage in the reader, alongside writing about it

**How it helps you:**
- The card was previously stuck on one passage forever, with no way to say "not that one"
- Most of a week's study happens before the group meets, so the passage is worth being able to just read

### The planner says what it does

**What changed:**
- "Add a gathering" is now "New gathering", and it schedules — creating one puts it on your group's next meeting date instead of in an undated pile
- The same for a church's sermons and a channel's studies, each keeping its own word
- The Ideas column is now the one place that makes something undated, using its own plus button
- Planners no longer claim to be read-only because of a paused church pilot when the group has no church at all
- A planner that genuinely is read-only says so in every view, including the calendar and series views, which previously just went quiet

**How it helps you:**
- One button, one outcome — pressing "New gathering" used to open a pane headed "New idea" whose button read "Add to ideas"
- You can tell whether you are allowed to change a plan without discovering it by trying

### What the group's notes start with

**What changed:**
- Choosing what a gathering's notes start with now shows you the shape you are handing out, rather than only its name
- Choices are grouped by where they come from — this space, or your church
- A group with no church is no longer offered church templates, or told to go and manage them

**How it helps you:**
- You can see what everyone will open before you publish it, instead of finding out from somebody else's note

### One thing that is now narrower

**What changed:**
- In a church-hosted room, reading the full teaching plan now takes a leader or the owner, matching how it already worked in every other group
- Members still see what is coming up next, which is the part that was ever meant for the room

**How it helps you:**
- A plan holds rough intentions as well as commitments; what your group sees is what has actually been decided

### Search results say what each one is

**What changed:**
- Every result in the Library's search now carries a mark saying what it is: a note, a folder, a Thread, a highlight, a book, a passage
- Browsing already did this. Typing used to take the marks away
- Notes were the worst of it, wearing no mark at all once they were mixed in with other kinds
- The marks show up in the lists that hold more than one kind: the results on Everything, and the "everywhere else" group underneath any other tab

**How it helps you:**
- A folder and a Thread that happen to share a name read as two plainly different rows again, rather than as the same result twice
- Nothing was ever duplicated — search has always returned each thing once — but until now there was no way to tell that by looking
