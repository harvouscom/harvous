Notes change. You rewrite a paragraph, cut a section you later want back, or open a note from last spring and wonder what it said before you tidied it.

Harvous has always kept the earlier versions of your notes. Until now there was no way to see them.

---

## How it works

### Open History from any note you wrote

In a note's ⋯ menu, choose History. You get the times you worked on that note, grouped by day, newest first. Pick one to read it exactly as it was.

### Restore, and change your mind

Restore puts the note back to how it looked then. What was there a moment ago is not lost: it becomes the newest entry in History, and the message that confirms the restore has an Undo.

### What Free and Plus see

Free shows the last 90 days of history. Harvous Plus shows all of it.

Nothing is deleted either way. History older than 90 days is kept for every account, and Plus is what lets you open it. If you upgrade, your older history is already there. If you cancel, it is hidden again, not removed.

A version counts as recent if it was replaced in the last 90 days. So if you edit a note today that you last touched a year ago, how it looked before today's edit is still yours to restore on Free.

### Older than 90 days

For history older than 90 days, Harvous keeps the last version from each time you worked on a note, rather than every autosave in between.

---

## What stays the same

- Your notes are never limited. This is about earlier versions of them, not the notes themselves.
- History is visible only to the person who wrote the note.
- Notes locked with a PIN do not show History on the web yet.
