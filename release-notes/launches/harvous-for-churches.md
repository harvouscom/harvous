# Harvous for churches

**Launched:** August 2026
**Shipped across:** v2.14.0–v2.33.0

Churches teach every week. What they teach mostly disappears — into a slide deck nobody reopens, a bulletin in a recycling bin, or a photo of a sermon point on someone's camera roll.

Harvous for churches is a way to close that gap. A church plans what it is teaching, publishes it to the people who attend, and those people study it in their own words, in the same app they already keep their notes in. What they write stays theirs.

---

## What a church can do

### Plan what you are teaching

The Planner is where a church's teaching lives before Sunday. It opens across the whole pane, with three ways to look at the same plan:

- A **board** you drag weeks around on
- A **calendar**, for when the date is the thing you are thinking about
- A **list**, for reading straight down the quarter

The sermon editor is a rail down the side, so you can see the quarter you are planning while you edit a week of it. Dragging a sermon to another Sunday is one motion, rather than opening it, changing the date, and saving. An idea with no date yet sits in a strip under the plan, waiting for one.

Each service carries a date, a title, a passage, and the series it belongs to. Passages are checked as you save, so a mistyped reference is caught before it reaches anyone's notes rather than turning into a dead link inside them.

### Plan a series as a series

A series used to be a label typed onto each week. It is a thing now: you open it, rename it, and see the whole run.

- Set how many weeks it runs, and the weeks you have not written yet appear as empty slots
- It tells you how many of its weeks are still to write
- An unwritten week is marked the same way everywhere it appears
- Renaming a series renames it everywhere, instead of quietly splitting it in two

A six-week series looks like six weeks from the day you plan it, rather than one week that keeps growing.

### Publish to the people who attend

Churches publish through ministry channels — one per ministry or study context, such as adult education, students, or a companion to the current sermon series. A ministry channel plans content rather than meetings, so its Planner asks for studies and devotionals instead of a gathering time.

Someone connected to your church browses the channels you publish and follows the ones they want. What they follow appears on their Home, under "From your church."

### Keep a shelf of what you teach from

Your church can keep the links and files it teaches from in one place.

- Staff choose who each resource is for: everyone in the church, or leaders only
- A resource can belong to the whole church, or to particular rooms, like Youth or the Tuesday study
- Anything on the shelf can be attached to a planned sermon or gathering

The commentary your pastor keeps recommending is somewhere findable. Leaders' notes and answer keys stay with leaders, without a second place to keep them. And if your church's plan lapses, the shelf stays readable — you do not lose what you have been given.

### Let the shelf be shaped by the people using it

Anyone connected to your church can suggest a resource. A pastor or admin reviews suggestions and decides what gets added. The talk that helped you can reach the rest of your church.

### Hand out the right amount of access

- A **pastor** sets what the church teaches and what notes start from
- A **teacher** reads the plan they teach from, and publishes to channels
- An **admin** manages the team
- A **volunteer** can be given leadership of one space, from that space's own people list — the person who actually runs the youth channel can publish to it without being added to church staff

Leadership is granted deliberately and can be taken back the same way. It stays narrow on purpose: one space, and nothing else in the church.

### Run on your own clock

A church sets its time zone and lists the services it actually holds, like 9:00 and 10:45 on Sunday. Service times show as the church's wall clock, never converted to the reader's. "This Sunday, 9:00 AM" means what your church means by it, even for someone reading three time zones away. A church with a morning and an evening service can plan both, instead of choosing one.

## What it looks like from a pew

If you have picked your church in Settings, Home shows what your church is teaching next — what it is called, and the series it belongs to.

"Start notes" makes a new note in My Home with the passage already set up as a scripture pill and your church's own note template already in the body. If you have already started notes for that service, the card opens them rather than making a second one. The card stays for a few days afterwards, so writing up Sunday on a Monday still works.

You arrive on Sunday already knowing the passage, and leave with notes in your own words rather than a photo of a slide. Because those notes are anchored to the passage, they come back to you later through Review, like anything else you write.

Nothing about church features appears for anyone who has not connected a church in Settings.

## What your church cannot see

This is the part worth being explicit about, because it is the part people are right to ask about.

Harvous never tells your church what you read, what you wrote, or whether you opened anything at all. There is no per-person reporting, and there is no way to ask for it.

What a church does get is an Engagement pane with counts only: how many people are connected, and how many follow each channel. Enough to know whether a ministry is reaching people. Not enough to know anything about you.

The single exception is a suggestion. Suggestions carry your name, because you chose to send one and a pastor cannot reply to it without knowing who it came from. That is the whole of it.

## Pricing

The Harvous plan for churches is 30 dollars a month, or 216 dollars a year. Paying yearly saves 40 percent.

Churches can start on a pilot and subscribe when they are ready. If a plan lapses, staff cannot add to the plan, but everything already planned stays visible and everyone keeps their notes.
