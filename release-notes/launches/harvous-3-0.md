# Harvous 3.0

**Launched:** September 2026
**Shipped across:** v2.88.0-v2.166.6

If you have used Harvous before, the first thing you will notice is a few names changed. Home is now Activity. The sidebar is now Search. Nothing moved, nothing was deleted — every note, every highlight, every Thread is exactly where you left it, under a name that says what it actually does.

Two real things came with the rename. Review brings your own study back to you on a schedule, and checks the answers that have one. Challenges turns something you have already written into a short guided path that ends with more of your own writing. Both are part of Harvous Plus. Alongside them: a way to try Harvous before you have an account, and a Bible reader that can now hold two translations open at once.

---

## What changed if you have been here before

**Home is Activity.** It is still the same feed — what you read, what you wrote, what is worth coming back to — under the name for what it does rather than a name that only made sense as a starting screen.

**The sidebar is Search.** Finding something now opens one panel rather than a column down the side. It looks across your notes, folders, Threads, highlights, Scripture and resources at once, keeps what you searched for recently, and lets you select several results and act on all of them together — move eight notes into a folder in one motion instead of eight.

**Recall is Suggestions.** Same shelf on your Activity view, offering a passage worth a second look or a note worth adding to. A plainer word for a plain thing.

## Review: your own study, brought back to you

Review keeps a quiet record of what you have been reading, marking, linking and writing about, and each day brings a few of those things back as a question — never more than three, and never a number telling you how many are waiting. There is nothing to turn on and no list to build by hand; it fills itself.

- A question is a verse, a note, or now a chapter you have actually read — not glanced at. It asks which verse belongs in a chapter, has you finish one from memory, put a few in the order they come, or say who appears in it.
- Where a question has a right answer, Harvous checks it and shows you what it checked. The result names the question, marks what you said, and shows the truth underneath — a full recap, not just a verdict.
- How well you hold something is a single plain word next to it: still learning, you know it, or slipping away. Never a percentage, never a streak.
- How it goes decides when it comes back: tomorrow if you needed to look, four days if you almost had it, two weeks if you had it clean. Three clean answers running and the gap starts widening on its own. Harvous can always tell you exactly when something returns.
- Every question is written by a person and filled in with your own material — your words, the verse's own words, or a curated reference index. Nothing here is written by a model, and nothing you write is read by one. Review checks facts; it does not grade what you think or how you say it.

## Challenges: a short path through what you already have

Start a challenge from a Thread you have been building, a note titled as a question, or a verse you want to keep. Each one is four or five steps: recall the central question, add the evidence, link one more passage and say why it belongs, name what is still unresolved, write a summary for your future self. Skip sits right beside Done on every step, and a path where you skipped something still finishes — nothing calls that a miss.

Every step leaves something real. A link step makes an actual connection between two notes. A summary step becomes an actual note in your library. There is no score, no streak, and no leaderboard anywhere in Challenges, on purpose.

## Try Harvous before you decide

You can now write a real note, highlight a real verse, and walk the getting-started checklist without making an account first. If you decide to sign up, everything you did as a guest comes with you — nothing is thrown away and nothing has to be redone. A guest never hits a wall that only an account can open; the one thing not offered yet is sharing, since that needs an identity to share as.

## Two translations, side by side

The Bible reader can now hold two translations of a chapter open at once, laid behind each other like paper, aligned verse by verse. On a phone the two versions take turns with a swipe instead of splitting the screen. And you can now keep a translation for offline reading — Settings, Translations shows what you have downloaded and how much room it takes.

## What stays exactly the same

Shared Spaces and Harvous for churches are not new in 3.0 — they are the reason 3.0 has something worth reviewing and studying together in the first place, and neither one changed shape here. If you have not seen the church side of Harvous, [the churches launch note](harvous-for-churches.md) is the whole story.

## Pricing

Harvous Plus is 7 dollars a month, or 49 dollars a year. Paying yearly saves 42 percent.

One subscription covers all of it: hosting a shared space, Review, and Challenges. Joining a space someone else hosts is free and always has been, and everything you have written stays yours to read whether or not you subscribe.

Forty-nine is seven sevens, the Jubilee arithmetic of Leviticus 25. It seemed like the right number for a product about returning to what you have already studied.

The first 99 people to subscribe pay 35 dollars for their first year and keep a founding badge on the account afterwards. It renews at the regular yearly price.

The Harvous plan for churches is unchanged at 30 dollars a month, or 216 dollars a year.

## Getting started

If you already have an account, there is nothing to do — Review and Challenges are already there, on your Activity view, waiting on your own study rather than on a button you have to find. If you do not have an account yet, you can start using Harvous right now and decide about an account later.
